# 04 — HCI and UX principles for live performance and high-pressure control

Research date: 2026-11-21. Scope: principles that should shape the Perform tab, Vibes, tap reactions, zones and safety features of the laser controller. Pain-point IDs P1–P8 are from the brief.

## 1. Summary

The research points one way. A live laser controller should behave like a small, fixed instrument, not like an editor. Performers do best with a few controls that never move or change meaning (Hick–Hyman, Cowan's 3–5 chunk limit, busking practice). Alternate functions should only last while a key is held (Raskin's quasimodes; Sellen et al. found these cut mode errors). Every press should show clearly what it changed, and changes the system made on its own should look different from changes the performer made (Schloss, Fels, Berthaut). The stop path must be one dedicated control that cannot be remapped and acts at once (QLab, FDA variance conditions, MIL-STD-1472). Alarms should be rare, tiered and believable (FAA 25.1322, Bliss). One finding should shape the whole design. US FDA laser-show variances require the operator to be able to see every beam path and the audience at all times. So the best UI is one the performer barely has to look at: dim, readable from 2 m, and echoed on the controller's LEDs. For preparation, field studies of DJs and lighting buskers agree: performers prepare a small, curated kit (a "crate", a "busk page") and improvise inside it. That is exactly what a "vibe" should be.

## 2. NIME and digital-instrument research

- **Mapping is the instrument.** The mapping layer "can define the very essence of an instrument" ([Hunt, Wanderley & Paradis, NIME 2002](https://nime.org/proc/nime2002_hunt), [PDF](https://nime.org/proceedings/2002/nime2002_088.pdf)). *So for this app:* tap reactions and role assignments are the instrument. Design them as carefully as the renderer.
- **One-to-many and many-to-many mappings are more engaging but slower to learn.** In Hunt & Kirk's tests, the multiparametric interface scored lower than plain sliders on the simplest tasks. It scored higher on complex tasks and improved over time; one-to-one sliders did not ([Hunt, Wanderley & Kirk, ICMC 2000](http://recherche.ircam.fr/anasyn/wanderle/Gestes/Externe/Hunt_Towards.pdf); [Hunt & Wanderley 2002](https://web.media.mit.edu/~rebklein/downloads/papers/Organised%20Sounds%20V7-n2/02-%20Mapping%20performer%20parameters%20to%20synthesis%20engines.pdf)). *So for this app:* multi-effect "impacts" (one press moves size, brightness and colour) are a good idea, but expect them to feel opaque at first. Show what each press moves.
- **Low entry fee, no ceiling on virtuosity, and tight timing.** Wessel & Wright set ≤10 ms action-to-result latency and ≤1 ms jitter ([CMJ 2002 PDF](https://cnmat.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/2002_problems-and-prospects-for-intimate-musical-control-of-computers.pdf)). Jack et al. found that 20 ms, or 10 ms ± 3 ms jitter, made percussive instruments worse, while 10 ms did not ([Audio Mostly 2016](https://andrewmcpherson.org/instrumentslab-data/data/andrew/jack_am2016.pdf)). *So for this app:* treat press-to-photon latency and jitter as a feature with a budget. Not verified: whether audio thresholds carry over exactly to light flashes.
- **Efficiency and the learning curve.** Jordà argues that a kalimba is a more "efficient" instrument than a piano for a beginner: easy at first but still rich ([NIME 2004 PDF](https://www.nime.org/proceedings/2004/nime2004_059.pdf)). *So for this app:* the five-pad Perform tab is the kalimba. The cue grid and timeline are the piano.
- **Constraints are a design tool.** Magnusson describes constraints as limits that "save time" and "minimize the performer's cognitive load" in real-time work ([CMJ 2010 PDF](https://cris.brighton.ac.uk/ws/files/268347/34.4.magnusson.pdf)). Gurevich et al. gave players a one-button instrument, and they still developed distinct personal styles ([NIME 2010 PDF](https://www.nime.org/proceedings/2010/nime2010_106.pdf)). *So for this app:* five fixed roles are not a limitation to apologise for. Variety should come from *which* vibe is loaded, not from more buttons.
- **Instrument vs controller vs score.** A digital instrument has three parts: a gestural controller, a mapping and a generator ([Wanderley & Depalle 2004, via Hunt & Wanderley](https://web.media.mit.edu/~rebklein/downloads/papers/Organised%20Sounds%20V7-n2/02-%20Mapping%20performer%20parameters%20to%20synthesis%20engines.pdf)). A "composed instrument" also contains pre-composed material ([Schnell & Battier, NIME 2002](https://nime.org/proc/nime2002_schnell/)). *So for this app:* MIDI, keyboard and gamepad are the controller. Tap reactions and zone patterns are the mapping. A vibe is a composed instrument. The timeline is a score. Keep those four ideas separate in the UI (helps P7).
- **Transparency for the performer and the audience.** Fels et al. frame expressivity as how transparent the mapping is to both the player and the audience ([Organised Sound 2002 PDF](https://web.media.mit.edu/~rebklein/downloads/papers/Organised%20Sounds%20V7-n2/03-%20Mapping%20transparency%20through%20metaphor-%20towards%20more%20expressive%20musical%20instruments.pdf)). Schloss: "Cause-and-effect is important… Magic in a performance is good. Too much magic is fatal!" ([JNMR 2003 PDF](https://web.uvic.ca/~aschloss/publications/JNMR02_Dilemma_of_the_Performer.pdf)). Reeves et al. classify interfaces as secretive, expressive, magical or suspenseful, depending on whether the manipulation and the effect are hidden or shown ([CHI 2005](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1054972.1055074)). *So for this app:* a HIT press should land on the beat with a visible, consistent effect. Hiding the button is fine (the "magical" strategy). Inconsistent results are not.
- **Automation hides the performer.** Visual augmentations helped audiences see the musician's influence "when parts of the music are automated" ([Berthaut et al., NIME 2015](https://nime.org/proc/hlimerick2015/index.html)). Rouages showed the audience which parts are played and which are automated ([NIME 2013](https://pure.ul.ie/en/publications/rouages-revealing-the-mechanisms-of-digital-musical-instruments-t/)). Explaining an instrument raised understanding but not enjoyment ([Bin et al., NIME 2016](https://www.academia.edu/26910541/Skip_the_Pre_Concert_Demo_How_Technical_Familiarity_and_Musical_Style_Affect_Audience_Response)). *So for this app:* the performer, rather than the audience, most needs to tell automatic changes (beat chases) from changes they caused (presses).
- **Mixed initiative and the sense of agency.** Horvitz: automation must add value, allow "efficient direct invocation and termination", and "minimize the cost of poor guesses" ([CHI 1999 PDF](https://erichorvitz.com/chi99horvitz.pdf)). Coyle et al.: up to a point, assistance keeps the user's sense of agency. Beyond it, agency drops "even if the computer correctly assists" ([CHI 2012 PDF](http://pokristensson.com/pubs/CoyleEtAlCHI2012.pdf); [summary](http://www.davidcoyle.org/the-experience-of-agency)). DJs and VJs are ambivalent about automated visuals and see them as a threat to artistic identity ([CHI 2020](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3313831.3376463)). *So for this app:* random pool picks and beat-driven zone patterns need an instant "freeze/hold" and a visible "auto" mark.

## 3. Cognitive load and decisions under time pressure

- **Hick–Hyman law.** Decision time grows with the log of the number of choices. Practice and stimulus–response compatibility reduce the slope ([Proctor & Schneider 2018 PDF](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~dws/pubs/ProctorSchneider_2018_QJEP.pdf); [Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/hicks-law)). *So for this app:* fewer live choices, in fixed positions that the performer can learn by heart.
- **Fitts's law.** Big, near targets are faster and cause fewer errors. NN/g recommends at least 1 cm × 1 cm for touch targets ([NN/g Fitts](http://nngroup.com/articles/fitts-law); [NN/g touch targets](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/)). MIL-STD-1472 asks for at least 13 mm between single-finger pushbuttons ([MIL-STD-1472F PDF](https://everyspec.com/MIL-STD/MIL-STD-1400-1499/download.php?spec=MIL-STD-1472F.027465.pdf)). *So for this app:* on-screen pads should be large, with BLACKOUT physically apart from the others. Applying the 13 mm hardware spacing to touch screens is my own extrapolation.
- **Working-memory limits.** The real limit is about 3–5 chunks, not 7 ([Cowan 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11515286)). *So for this app:* during a show, the performer should never need to hold more than about four concepts in mind (P7).
- **Recognition over recall.** Show the options instead of making users remember them ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/recognition-and-recall)). *So for this app:* label pads with what they do *now*, for example "HIT · pool: 6 cues".
- **Mode errors and capture errors.** Norman classifies mode errors as misjudging the situation, and capture errors as a familiar sequence taking over ([Norman 1981](https://www.academia.edu/57625630/Categorization_of_action_slips)). NN/g: show the active mode with at least two indicators, and avoid modes "in critical scenarios" ([NN/g modes](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modes)). *So for this app:* a latched "zone target" or "edit" mode on the Perform tab is a hazard (P6, P8).
- **Quasimodes.** Raskin defines a quasimode as a mode that lasts only while the user keeps acting, like holding Shift ([summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mode_(user_interface))). Sellen, Kurtenbach & Buxton found that a non-latching pedal prevented mode errors better than a latching one ([HCI 1992 PDF](http://billbuxton.com/ModeErrorsHCI.pdf)). NN/g: spring-loaded modes "are a major exception to the rule that modes cause usability problems" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/spring-loaded-modes)). *So for this app:* "hold SHIFT + pad" for alternate actions; never toggle.
- **Situation awareness.** Endsley's three levels are perceive, comprehend and project ([Human Factors 1995](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/001872095779049543)). Her design principles include "support global SA", "make modes and system states salient" and "avoid the proliferation of automation modes" ([Endsley 2024 slides](https://www.flextechchair.org/FTSpringSchool2024/ewExternalFiles/Endsley%20-%20%20H-AI%20teams%20HCI%20workshop%205-2024.pdf)). *So for this app:* one strip should show all projectors (level 1), with an "overloaded / dropped / masked" verdict (level 2) and "next beat step" (level 3).

## 4. Glanceable, dark-environment UI

- **Text size by distance.** Critical text should subtend at least 16 arc-minutes, with 20–24 preferred ([FAA HF-STD-001B PDF](https://hf.tc.faa.gov/publications/2016-12-human-factors-design-standard/full_text.pdf)). For coloured characters, MIL-STD-1472F asks for at least 21′, with 30′ preferred ([PDF](https://everyspec.com/MIL-STD/MIL-STD-1400-1499/download.php?spec=MIL-STD-1472F.027465.pdf)). Its label table gives 9.4 mm at 1–2 m and 18 mm at 2–4 m ([MIL-STD-1472H PDF](https://dn790000.ca.archive.org/0/items/mil-std-1472-g-chg-1/MIL-STD-1472H.pdf)). *So for this app:* at 2 m, 16′ works out to about 9.3 mm cap height and 30′ to about 17.5 mm (my arithmetic). Pixel sizes depend on the screen's pixel density.
- **Colour.** Never use colour as the only cue. Interface parts and their states need 3:1 contrast ([WCAG 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color); [WCAG 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast)). The Okabe–Ito palette is designed to stay distinguishable for all common colour-vision types ([CUD](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/)). *So for this app:* role colours should come from Okabe–Ito and be paired with an icon or letter. The laser's own output colours are content, not UI coding.
- **Night mode is a trade-off.** For normal vision, light mode usually reads better ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode)). But in low light, long-wavelength (red/amber) displays preserve night vision, and light-on-dark text read better ([Mantiuk et al. 2009 PDF](https://vccimaging.org/Publications/Mantiuk2009DCN/Mantiuk2009DCN.pdf); [Rempel et al. 2011](https://library.imaging.org/cic/articles/19/1/art00039)). A US Navy review found red's advantage small at low intensity, and red distorts colour coding; it recommended dim white ([Luria & Kobus 1984, mirror](https://dnstool.it-help.tech/static/references/Luria-Kobus-Red-White-Light-Dark-Adaptation-1984.pdf); [J. Navigation 2012](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-navigation/article/nighttime-lookout-duty-the-role-of-ambient-light-levels-and-dark-adaptation/6EA9B79C787F32DA9978A095701CD654)). *So for this app:* use a dim, dark-background show mode with a brightness control and no big white areas. Do not use an all-red mode, because it would break colour-coded roles.
- **Calm, peripheral status.** Good tools move "from the periphery of our attention, to the center, and back" ([Weiser & Brown 1995](https://calmtech.com/papers/designing-calm-technology)). A dashboard puts the most important information "on a single screen so it can be monitored at a glance" ([Few](https://www.perceptualedge.com/articles/Whitepapers/Rich_Data_Poor_Data.pdf)). *So for this app:* use one fixed status strip and never scroll the live view.
- **LED feedback on controllers.** On Push and Launchpad, a flashing pad means queued, a pulsing pad means playing, and the clip's colour is shown on both hardware and screen. Holding "Session" switches modes only while held ([Push 3 manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/push/manual); [Launchpad Pro MK3 guide](https://files.kraftmusic.com/media/ownersmanual/Novation_Launchpad_Pro_MK3_User_Guide.pdf)). *So for this app:* mirror pad state to MIDI controller LEDs using the same flash/pulse grammar. That lets the eyes stay off the screen.

## 5. Safety-critical and show-control patterns

- **Tiered alerts.** In aviation, red means warning (act now), amber means caution (act soon) and advisories use neither red nor green. Alerts must "minimize the effects of false and nuisance alerts", and if an alert is suppressed the crew must be told clearly ([14 CFR 25.1322](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-25.1322); [FAA HF guidance](https://hfcc.dot.gov/publications/docs/GeneralGuidance/zz_FAA_GeneralGuidanceDoc_Chapter_04_Section_03.html)). Master alerts sit in the primary field of view, within about ±15° ([FAA](https://hfcc.dot.gov/publications/docs/GeneralGuidance/zz_FAA_GeneralGuidanceDoc_Chapter_04_Section_01.html)).
- **Alarm fatigue.** People respond to alarms about as often as the alarms turn out to be right. If an alarm is right 10% of the time, people respond to it only a little more than 10% of the time ([Edworthy review of Bliss](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3628049)). Only severe alerts should interrupt ([AHRQ](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/alert-fatigue)). *So for this app:* the load meter and the stuck-key watchdog must not cry wolf.
- **Guards without delay.** Protect controls using location, recess, cover, interlock or resistance. But protection "shall not preclude operation within the time required". Mushroom buttons are reserved for emergency stop. Dead-man controls should shut down to a safe state ([MIL-STD-1472H PDF](https://dn790000.ca.archive.org/0/items/mil-std-1472-g-chg-1/MIL-STD-1472H.pdf); [FAA HFDS ch.6](https://hf.tc.faa.gov/hfds/download-hfds/hfds_pdfs/Ch6_Controls_and_visual_indicators.pdf)). A critical control "should be dedicated to that function only" ([NASA KSC-YA-5436](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20020039027/downloads/20020039027.pdf)).
- **The "go/panic" model.** In QLab, a single Esc always panics, a double Esc always hard-stops, and this "cannot be changed", so that "any person familiar with QLab can confidently use the escape key" ([QLab 5 settings](https://qlab.app/docs/v5/fundamentals/workspace-settings); [shortcuts](https://reference.qlab.app/docs/v5/general/keyboard-shortcuts)). Double-GO protection ignores repeats within a set time, flashes red to show a command was ignored, and can require key-up before re-arming. *So for this app:* adopt these patterns for BLACKOUT and the stuck-key watchdog. One difference: QLab's panic fades out, while laser safety requires an *immediate* stop (next bullet).
- **Laser rules as they affect the UI.** FDA variance conditions say the operator must "be located where all propagating beam paths, their terminations, and the audience can be directly observed at all times". They must also be able to "immediately terminate" emission with "readily accessible controls", and projector output must not exceed what the effect needs ([FDA variance example](https://downloads.regulations.gov/FDA-2026-V-1735-0003/attachment_2.pdf)). Audience scanning requires a scan-fail safeguard ([FDA Form 3640](https://www.fda.gov/media/72658/download)). ILDA says scan-fail cutoff should happen within 10 ms, preferably 1 ms ([ILDA paper](https://www.ilda.com/resources/Safety/audience-scanning_overview_latest.pdf)). It describes software "beam attenuation maps" and requires an E-stop held by a trained operator ([ILDA](https://ilda.com/audiencescanningsafety.htm)). ILDA defers to ANSI Z136 and IEC 60825 for exposure limits ([ILDA basics](https://www.ilda.com/safety-basics.htm)). The UK HSE calls for "well-practised emergency shutdown procedures" ([HSE INDG224](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/INDG224.htm)). *So for this app:* the UI must let the operator look at the room. The software blackout is not the E-stop and should never be presented as one.
- **Error prevention (poka-yoke).** A control-type device, which physically prevents the error, is stronger than a warning ([ASQ](https://asq.org/quality-resources/mistake-proofing); [Grout, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2464876)). *So for this app:* safety zones should clip the output, not just warn.
- **Confirm vs undo.** Frequent confirmations train people to click through them. Prefer undo for anything reversible ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-mistakes)). Use modals only to prevent "destructive, irreversible" errors ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog)).

## 6. Setup and learnability

- **Progressive disclosure.** Show "only a few of the most important options" first, and make the route to advanced options obvious ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure)). *So for this app:* the Perform tab shows roles. Pools, reaction curves and maps are one level down.
- **Wizards vs checklists.** Wizards suit "novice users or infrequent processes (e.g., configuration or setup)" but annoy repeat users ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards)). *So for this app:* venue setup is infrequent but repeated by experts. Use a resumable checklist with a wizard-style first run.
- **Empty states teach.** "Do not default to totally empty states". Say what would appear and how to fill it ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design)). *So for this app:* an empty hit pool should say "Drag cues here or press ⇧ on a cue" instead of doing nothing silently (P6).
- **Explain automatic behaviour.** "Make clear why the system did what it did" was one of the most-violated of the human–AI guidelines ([Amershi et al., CHI 2019](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3290605.3300233)). "Why" explanations improved understanding and trust ([Lim, Dey & Avrahami, CHI 2009](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~byl/publications/lim_chi09.pdf)). *So for this app:* add a one-line "last change: zone chase step 3 (beat)" readout.
- **Preview before commit.** ETC Eos "Blind" edits recorded data "without affecting what is currently in Live". The background colour changes and the word "Blind" appears ([ETC L1 workbook](http://www.etcconnect.com/uploadedFiles/Main_Site/Documents/Public/Video_Tutorial/Eos_Family_L1_Essentials_v3.3.pdf); [Eos help](https://www.etcconnect.com/WebDocs/Controls/EosFamilyOnlineHelp/en/Content/04_System_Basics/Important_Concepts.htm)). *So for this app:* changing a zone map or vibe live should be previewable, with a distinct background.
- **Natural mapping.** Lay controls out in the same pattern as the things they control ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/natural-mappings)). *So for this app:* projector tiles should be ordered left to right exactly as the rig is (P4, P5).

## 7. Field studies of DJs, VJs and lighting operators

- **DJs prepare small crates even with unlimited libraries.** They collect, prepare crates, then improvise from them in response to the crowd ([Ahmed, Benford & Crabtree, CHI 2012 PDF](https://people.cs.nott.ac.uk/pszaxc/work/CHI12.pdf); [Benford blog](https://interactions.acm.org/blog/view/digging-the-crates-how-djs-improvise-like-banjo-players)). Creative DJs describe "planning to improvise" and being "ready to throw everything out of the window" ([Munro et al. 2023 PDF](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/82809/1/Munro_etal_JDoC_2023_The_information_behaviour_of_creative_DJs.pdf)). *So for this app:* a vibe is a crate. Making one must be quick (P2).
- **VJs.** Rhythmic match to the music is "a defining feature of a good VJ set". Bright, white visuals "only light up the room" ([Engström et al. 2008 PDF](https://docbox.etsi.org/STF/Archive/STF322_HF_EC_3G_MobileUserInterface/STFworkarea/FoldersFiles/MobileHIC%202008%20Proceedings/papers/p157-engstrom.pdf)). VJs value visible interaction and quick "load clips in; bang gone" tools ([Hook thesis 2013 PDF](https://jonhook.co.uk/publications/jonhookthesis_comp.pdf)).
- **Lighting buskers.** Build stacks of "building blocks" by parameter type. Use faders for fixture groups by type or location. Keep tap-tempo chases ready. Repeat the chorus look ([Schiller, LSA 2024 PDF](http://www.lightingandsoundamerica.com/reprint/Busking.pdf)). Keep a "lifesaver" macro that returns to a base cue ([ETC/Al-Alami](https://blog.etcconnect.com/2017/04/organized-improvisation-live-concert-busking-with-eos)) and a "punt look" ([Chauvet](https://chauvetprofessional.com/tech-talk-how-to-master-the-art-of-busking)). "Console screens are not supposed to be part of the show… you want to be looking at the stage" ([Bullock](https://www.chauvetprofessional.com/news/jason-bullock-the-calculus-of-light)). Four options per attribute family may be enough, because "fewer things to get lost amongst" ([On Stage Lighting](https://www.onstagelighting.co.uk/console-programming/ultimate-busk-file-setup)). These are practitioner sources, not peer-reviewed.
- **Gap.** I found no ethnographic study of laser-show operators specifically (not verified that none exists).

## 8. Design rules for this app

1. **A performance control never opens a dialog, menu or modal.** Test: no Perform-tab input creates a window or popover. (P1, P8) [NN/g modals](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog), [Bullock](https://www.chauvetprofessional.com/news/jason-bullock-the-calculus-of-light)
2. **At most five role pads and two faders live, each fixed in position, label and colour across every vibe.** Test: switching vibes moves no pad. (P1, P7) [Proctor & Schneider](https://web.ics.purdue.edu/~dws/pubs/ProctorSchneider_2018_QJEP.pdf), [Magnusson](https://cris.brighton.ac.uk/ws/files/268347/34.4.magnusson.pdf)
3. **Live labels readable at 2 m:** cap height ≥ 9.5 mm, and ≥ 17.5 mm where colour carries meaning. (P1) [FAA HF-STD-001B](https://hf.tc.faa.gov/publications/2016-12-human-factors-design-standard/full_text.pdf), [MIL-STD-1472F](https://everyspec.com/MIL-STD/MIL-STD-1400-1499/download.php?spec=MIL-STD-1472F.027465.pdf)
4. **Touch pads ≥ 1 cm square (aim much larger). BLACKOUT is separated from other pads by ≥ 13 mm, or placed in a different screen region.** (P1, P8) [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/touch-target-size/), [MIL-STD-1472F](https://everyspec.com/MIL-STD/MIL-STD-1400-1499/download.php?spec=MIL-STD-1472F.027465.pdf)
5. **No state is shown by colour alone.** Every state has a second cue (icon, fill, motion or text) and ≥ 3:1 contrast. Role colours come from Okabe–Ito. (P1, P6) [WCAG 1.4.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color), [WCAG 1.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast), [CUD](https://jfly.uni-koeln.de/color/)
6. **One motion grammar everywhere: steady = ready, pulse = running, flash = queued/armed. Mirror it to controller LEDs.** (P1, P6) [Push 3 manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/push/manual)
7. **Alternate functions are quasimodes. They are active only while a modifier is held, and releasing always restores the default.** (P1, P7) [Sellen et al.](http://billbuxton.com/ModeErrorsHCI.pdf), [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/spring-loaded-modes)
8. **Any latched state that changes what a pad does gets two persistent indicators visible from 2 m.** Better still, avoid such states during Perform. (P6, P7) [NN/g modes](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modes)
9. **BLACKOUT is one dedicated, non-remappable control with the same key on every input device. It acts immediately (no fade), and works when the show is locked and when the UI is busy.** (P8) [QLab](https://qlab.app/docs/v5/fundamentals/workspace-settings), [FDA variance](https://downloads.regulations.gov/FDA-2026-V-1735-0003/attachment_2.pdf), [NASA KSC](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20020039027/downloads/20020039027.pdf)
10. **Leaving blackout uses a different gesture from entering it (e.g. hold ≥ 1 s). Guards may slow the restart, never the stop.** (P8) [MIL-STD-1472H §5.1.1.7](https://dn790000.ca.archive.org/0/items/mil-std-1472-g-chg-1/MIL-STD-1472H.pdf)
11. **Triggers are debounced. Repeats within N ms are ignored, and a flash shows "received but ignored". Key-up is required before re-arming, and a stuck key releases to a safe state.** (P6, P8) [QLab](https://qlab.app/docs/v5/fundamentals/workspace-settings), [FAA HFDS dead-man](https://hf.tc.faa.gov/hfds/download-hfds/hfds_pdfs/Ch6_Controls_and_visual_indicators.pdf)
12. **Press-to-output latency target ≤ 10 ms with low jitter. It is measured, and the UI shows a warning when exceeded.** (P3, P6) [Wessel & Wright](https://cnmat.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/2002_problems-and-prospects-for-intimate-musical-control-of-computers.pdf), [Jack et al.](https://andrewmcpherson.org/instrumentslab-data/data/andrew/jack_am2016.pdf)
13. **Every pad shows what it moves, e.g. "size + bright + colour", and each zone or projector it touches flashes in the pad's colour on press.** (P3, P5) [Fels et al.](https://web.media.mit.edu/~rebklein/downloads/papers/Organised%20Sounds%20V7-n2/03-%20Mapping%20transparency%20through%20metaphor-%20towards%20more%20expressive%20musical%20instruments.pdf), [Berthaut 2015](https://nime.org/proc/hlimerick2015/index.html)
14. **Automatic changes (beat chases, pool picks, pattern steps) are marked "auto", and a one-line readout says why the last change happened.** (P3, P5, P6) [Amershi G11](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3290605.3300233), [Lim et al.](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~byl/publications/lim_chi09.pdf)
15. **One action freezes all automation (hold the current look). Another single action resumes it.** (P1, P5) [Horvitz](https://erichorvitz.com/chi99horvitz.pdf), [Coyle et al.](http://pokristensson.com/pubs/CoyleEtAlCHI2012.pdf)
16. **Thumbnails show what is actually sent, after zones, maps and safety masks. A dropped or overloaded projector is marked in its own tile.** (P6) [Endsley](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1518/001872095779049543), [Few](https://www.perceptualedge.com/articles/Whitepapers/Rich_Data_Poor_Data.pdf)
17. **A control that would do nothing is visibly disabled and states why, e.g. "pool empty".** (P6) [NN/g empty states](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design)
18. **Projector tiles follow physical left-to-right order, and each has an "identify" action that blinks that physical laser.** (P4, P5) [NN/g natural mapping](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/natural-mappings)
19. **Live edits to vibes and zone maps happen in a preview area with a distinct background and label, and are committed with one explicit action.** (P2, P5, P6) [ETC Eos Blind](http://www.etcconnect.com/uploadedFiles/Main_Site/Documents/Public/Video_Tutorial/Eos_Family_L1_Essentials_v3.3.pdf)
20. **Alerts have three tiers: red = safety/stop, amber = degraded (overload, lost projector), neutral = info. At most one red alert is shown at a time, and nuisance alerts are tracked and cut.** (P6, P8) [14 CFR 25.1322](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/section-25.1322), [Edworthy/Bliss](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3628049)
21. **Reversible edits use undo, not confirmation. Confirmation is only for irreversible deletes and names the object.** (P2, P4) [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/user-mistakes)
22. **Safety zones and audience-scan limits are applied last in the output chain. They cannot be switched off from Perform, and their state is always visible.** (P8) [ILDA](https://ilda.com/audiencescanningsafety.htm), [ASQ poka-yoke](https://asq.org/quality-resources/mistake-proofing)
23. **Show mode is dim and dark-backgrounded with adjustable brightness and no large white fields, so the operator can watch beams and audience.** (P1, P8) [FDA variance](https://downloads.regulations.gov/FDA-2026-V-1735-0003/attachment_2.pdf), [Rempel et al.](https://library.imaging.org/cic/articles/19/1/art00039)
24. **A playable vibe can be made from a cue selection in ≤ 1 minute, with roles auto-filled by defaults.** (P2) [Ahmed et al.](https://people.cs.nott.ac.uk/pszaxc/work/CHI12.pdf), [Schiller](http://www.lightingandsoundamerica.com/reprint/Busking.pdf)
25. **The live view shows at most four concepts at once (e.g. vibe, roles, zone map, intensity). Everything else lives in setup.** (P7) [Cowan](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11515286), [NN/g progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure)

## 9. Heuristic checklist (yes/no, apply to a mockup)

1. Can every live action be done without opening anything?
2. Does each pad keep the same place, colour and label when the vibe changes?
3. From 2 m in a dark room, can you read every live label and tell every pad state?
4. Is each state shown by at least two cues, not colour alone?
5. Do the screen and controller LEDs use the same steady/pulse/flash meaning?
6. Are all alternate functions hold-to-use (quasimodes)?
7. Is BLACKOUT unique, hard to hit by accident, impossible to remap and instant?
8. After any press, can you see which zones and projectors it moved?
9. Can you tell an automatic change from one you caused, and see why it happened?
10. Does each projector tile show real output, and mark masked, overloaded or dropped states?
11. Does every inactive control say why it is inactive?
12. Is there at most one red element, and is it only used for safety?
13. Could the operator run a song while looking mostly at the room?
14. Is every reversible change undoable without a confirm box?
15. Is projector order on screen the same as in the rig?

## 10. Open questions

- **Is BLACKOUT an effect or a safety stop?** QLab separates panic (a fade) from hard stop. Laser rules require an immediate stop and a readily accessible terminate control. Should the creative BLACKOUT pad be separate from a safety stop that is tied to the hardware interlock?
- **Do audio latency thresholds (10 ms, ≤1–3 ms jitter) hold for light hits synced to music?** Not verified. This needs a small test.
- **How many effects can one press move before it stops being legible?** Hunt & Kirk suggest rich mappings pay off later but confuse at first.
- **How much automation is too much?** Coyle et al. found that correct assistance can still lower the sense of agency. Where is that line for beat-driven zone patterns?
- **Dark adaptation vs colour coding.** Red night modes protect vision but break colour roles. Is dim white plus Okabe–Ito enough in real venues?
- **Should the audience be able to read causality?** Schloss warns that "too much magic is fatal". Should some laser reactions be designed to read as "played"?
- **Gamepad, keyboard and MIDI all have different layouts.** Can roles keep one consistent spatial position across all of them?
- **No laser-operator field study was found.** A short observation study of a few laser jockeys would test these rules.
