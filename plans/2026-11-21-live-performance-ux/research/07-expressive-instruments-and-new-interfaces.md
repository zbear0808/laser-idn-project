# 07 — Expressive instruments and new interfaces: what they teach a light instrument

Date: 2026-11-21. Scope: research only. Items marked "not verified" were not confirmed from a primary source.

## 1. Summary

The best expressive instruments share a few traits. One touch carries several continuous dimensions, such as strike, press, glide, slide and lift. Latency is low and, more importantly, stable: about 10 ms or less, with jitter of 1 ms or less. Some things snap to a grid and others stay free. The player feels a physical threshold before a second layer opens. Light or haptic feedback appears where the hand is. The instrument also forgives mistakes, through hit windows, pitch "rounding", or an engine that keeps the beat while the player shapes intensity. History is directly relevant. Jarre's laser harp makes light the instrument. Fischinger's Lumigraph let the body press shapes into a sheet of light. Greenewalt built her colour organ around fine intensity control. Henke's Lumière drives sound and lasers from the same control signals. Games add charge-and-release mechanics (Guitar Hero's star power), scoring based on motion rather than timing (Beat Saber), and audio-first cueing (Rhythm Heaven). For our app, the clearest move is from pads that trigger looks to pads that *reveal and shape* beat-locked looks. Each pad would gain pressure, slide and lift dimensions, plus looping and motion-record layers on top.

## 2. Findings by group

### 2.1 MPE and pressure surfaces

- **ROLI Seaboard / Blocks.** ROLI names five dimensions of touch. Strike is note-on velocity, Press is aftertouch, Glide is pitch bend, Slide is CC74 and Lift is release velocity. Techniques include glide vibrato, slide tremolo and the "pull-off", a fast lift that triggers a release sound. Touch Faders set the sensitivity of each dimension. Wikipedia cites latency of 24 ms over USB and 30 ms over Bluetooth. https://support.roli.com/support/solutions/articles/36000019157-what-is-5d-touch- , https://support.roli.com/en/support/solutions/articles/36000026996-seaboard-playing-techniques , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ROLI_Seaboard
  - ROLI's sound designer says the dimensions are coupled. You cannot Lift without passing through Press, so "expression curves" matter most. https://www.kvraudio.com/interviews/expressing-yourself-with-sound-an-interview-with-roli-sound-designer-rafael-szaban-54704
- **LinnStrument.** It has 200 (or 128) pads, each sensing velocity, X, Y, Z and release. Rows are tuned in fourths, so the layout is isomorphic: one chord shape works in every key. The natural notes are lit, the Cs in blue, and the C pads have Braille bumps. Typical latency is 2 ms and the lightest detected touch is 50 g. https://www.rogerlinndesign.com/linnstrument , https://www.rogerlinndesign.com/support/support-linnstrument-fourths-layout
- **Expressive E Osmose.** Each key moves on three axes. The first dip is about 9 mm of continuous pressure. Past a "clearly detectable pressure point" an aftertouch layer begins. Keys also rock sideways for per-note bend. Players report triggering bends by accident, so the instrument has a "stabilization" setting. https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/expressive-e-osmose , https://expressivee.happyfox.com/kb/article/250-sensitivity-menu
- **Haken Continuum.** It is a smooth neoprene surface with 0.33 ms response. Pitch resolution is finer than 0.1 cent. A software "rounding" feature pulls pitch onto a scale, and the player sets its amount and timing in real time. The maker says it takes years to master. https://www.hakenaudio.com/continuum-details , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuum_Fingerboard
- **Sensel Morph.** About 20,000 force sensors detect 5 g to 5 kg. Latency is 8 ms at 125 Hz, or 2 ms in a 500 Hz mode used for drum overlays. Swappable magnetic overlays change the layout. https://sensel.github.io/morph-docs/morph
- **Erae Touch / Erae 2.** It is a force-sensing surface with RGB LEDs underneath and a drag-and-drop layout editor. X and Y can be absolute (where you land matters) or relative (only movement matters). Latency is under 1 ms. https://www.embodme.com/erae , https://embodme.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/80001017320-midi-config-expressivity-control-change
- **Joué Play.** Silicone pads clip onto a pressure sensor and are identified by RFID. Each pad sends X, Y and pressure. SOS found the vibrato "a little difficult to control" and noted there is no visual feedback of control state. https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/joue-board-modules
- **Ableton Push 3.** It has 64 MPE pads with pressure, slide and per-note bend. "In Tune Location: Finger" means wherever you first land is in tune, and only later movement bends the note. "In Tune Width" sets a 0–20 mm dead zone. https://www.ableton.com/en/push/manual

### 2.2 Grid and generative instruments

- **monome grid.** "By default does nothing." Its LEDs are *decoupled* from its keys, so software can show state that has nothing to do with the current press. It has 16 brightness levels, and the manual notes that "a dim light could signal a modifier key whereas a bright light could signal a performative key". https://monome.org/docs/grid , https://monome.org/docs/grid/grid-norns . Norns and Teletype were not studied in depth (not verified).
- **Yamaha Tenori-on (Toshio Iwai).** It is a 16×16 LED button grid with six modes: Score (step grid), Random (a light travels the polygon you pressed), Draw (records your finger path), Bounce (a ball falls from where you press and sounds when it hits the floor, so a higher press gives a slower repeat), Push, and Solo. SOS said Bounce "isn't especially controllable". https://recherche.ircam.fr/equipes/temps-reel/nime06/proc/nime2006_172.pdf , https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/yamaha-tenori
- **Eno & Chilvers, Bloom.** A tap plays a note and draws a spreading circle. The note repeats and fades over a minute or two. If you stop playing, the app "evolves" on its own. In Bloom: 10 Worlds, pausing is rewarded with more variety. CDM found the tapping "only barely interactive". https://www.generativemusic.com/bloom.html , https://tapeop.com/interviews/137/peter-chilvers , https://cdm.link/hands-on-with-bloom-new-generative-iphone-app-by-eno-and-chilvers/
- **Fugue Machine.** Up to 4 playheads (8 in Rubato) run through one melody. Each can have its own speed, direction, start step, inversion and transpose. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fugue_Machine
- **Patterning.** It is a circular drum sequencer. Each ring can have a different length (1–512 steps), which makes polymeter easy. Swiping outward on a step raises its velocity. https://www.olympianoiseco.com/apps/patterning-3 , https://www.factmag.com/2015/08/23/patterning-drum-machine-app-ipad
- **Artiphon Orba.** A palm-sized dome with gestures: Tap, Press (finger contact area), Radiate (slide from centre to edge, mapped to brightness), Vibrato, Tilt, Spin, Move, Shake and Bump. It has a haptic engine and a four-track looper. https://www.bhphotovideo.com/lit_files/1203820.pdf
- **Reactable (Jordà et al.).** Tangible pucks on a lit table. The table draws the actual waveforms flowing between pucks. Björk toured with it. https://www.upf.edu/web/mtg/reactable , http://reactable.com/experience
- **Smule Ocarina (Ge Wang).** Blowing into the mic sets intensity, four touch holes set pitch, and tilt sets vibrato depth and rate. The holes animate on touch "to compensate for lack of tactile feedback". https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~ge/publish/ocarina-nime2009.pdf , https://www.icmc14-smc14.net/images/proceedings/OS21-B02-PrinciplesofVisualDesign.pdf
- Ableton Note, Playtronica and Bebot were not researched (not verified).

### 2.3 Gesture and body instruments

- **Theremin.** One hand sets pitch by distance from the vertical antenna. The other sets volume above the loop antenna. There is no physical reference, so the player relies only on the ear. Experts use "aerial fingering". Some models have a pitch-preview headphone output. Resting the elbow gives a steady pivot. https://www.liquisearch.com/theremin/performance_technique , https://cdn.inmusicbrands.com/Moog/Theremini/Theremini%20Manual.pdf
- **Jean-Michel Jarre's laser harp.** Breaking a beam triggers a cue. According to Pangolin, moving the hand *up or down within the beam* adds further effects through Beyond scripting. The harp talks to Beyond and FB4, and the light can also be run from a grandMA2. The open-source Harpelaser senses hand height for pitch bend and up to 3 CCs. It changes a beam's colour 400 ms after the note and has a "vibrating string" effect. https://plsn.com/articles/video-production/jean-michel-jarre-2017-spectacles-add-to-an-epic-career , https://www.harpelaser.com/
  - Claim, not verified: concert footage suggests that "striking the same beam produces different notes", meaning the harp may trigger the *next correct note* whichever beam is broken. https://www.chemeurope.com/en/encyclopedia/Laser_harp.html
- **Mi.Mu gloves (Imogen Heap).** Each glove has 8 flex sensors and a wrist IMU. Glover offers three input types. *Movements* are continuous values such as pitch, yaw, roll and finger bend. *Events* are discrete, such as drum hits. *Qualifiers* are states, such as postures. Up to 9 postures can be trained per hand, about 10 examples each, but fewer is better. The docs say "Fist tends to be one of the fastest and most reliable postures: use it to map things that require precise timing." https://mimugloves.com/documentation/glove-device-inputs/ , https://mimugloves.com/documentation/postures . Heap: buttons and dials are "not very exciting for me or the audience". https://www.dezeen.com/2014/03/31/imogen-heap-gloves-mini-frontiers-movie
- **Laetitia Sonami, lady's glove.** It carries Hall-effect sensors, bend strips, a pressure pad, ultrasonic distance sensors and accelerometers. Her definition: a controller "becomes an instrument when the software starts reflecting and adapting the limitations and possibilities of the controller". She describes performing with it as "bull riding". https://sonami.net/portfolio/items/ladys-glove , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laetitia_Sonami
- **Michel Waisvisz, The Hands (STEIM, 1984).** Two hand-mounted plates with keys, tilt switches and an ultrasonic sensor that measures the distance between the hands. Mercury switches turn a 12-key grid into an 8-octave instrument. His first verdict was "a bit disappointed... because it was so complex". His key quote: "Machines are precise with numbers, but the human hand is more precise with musical time." https://www.ul.ie/sites/default/files/scieng/TheHands.pdf , https://ebrary.net/41799/education/experimental_phase
- **Eigenharp Alpha.** It has 120 keys that sense pressure and two-axis rocking, sampled at 2000 Hz per key. It also has a breath pipe and two strips. Latency is under 7 ms end to end. https://www.eigenlabs.com/product/alpha
- **Genki Wave ring.** Inputs are tilt, pan, roll, vibrato, a "tap" (finger flick) and 3 buttons. Latency is 7–20 ms over Bluetooth LE and under 1 ms with a dedicated receiver. A reviewer found the tap "a little twitchy". The middle button pauses output so you can sneeze or grab a drink. https://docs.genki.is/general/faq/wave-for-music/ , https://waveformmagazine.com/waveform-reviews/wave-wavefront-genki-instruments
- **Webcam hand tracking (MediaPipe).** Inference takes about 13–25 ms per frame at around 30 fps. Reported end-to-end latency is under 100 ms. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.14125 , https://nime.org/proceedings/2026/nime2026_153.pdf . MiMU now links Glover to MediaPipe webcam tracking. https://nime.org/proc_music/nime2026_music_45/
- Leap Motion, Kinect, Myo and Wii-remote instruments were not studied directly (not verified). The Enhancia Neova ring company stopped trading in 2022 (forum report, not verified).

### 2.4 Drums and percussion as controllers

- **Sensory Percussion (Sunhouse).** A contactless sensor plus machine learning identifies the *zone* of each hit: center, edge, rimshot, rim tip or shoulder, cross-stick, stickshot and others. That is up to 10 zones on a snare. It also measures velocity, *timbre* (a smooth center-to-edge scale) and *speed*, meaning hit density, so a buzz roll can sound different from single strokes. Plain triggers respond "a few ms faster" because they skip timbre analysis. DRUM! measured about 98% zone accuracy after training. https://help.sunhou.se/v2/software-overview/software-concepts , https://help.sunhou.se/v2/glossary , https://drummagazine.com/gear-review-is-sensory-percussion-the-future-of-drum-triggering
- **Live looping.** The BOSS RC-505 has five stereo tracks, each with record/overdub/play, stop and a fader, plus undo/redo and a count-in. Marc Rebillet starts from nothing and keeps his mistakes. https://www.boss.info/global/products/rc-505/ , https://loopingguitar.com/en/artists/marc-rebillet . Ed Sheeran's small 2-track BOSS RC-30 is mentioned in https://musictech.com/reviews/boss-rc-505-review . Reggie Watts was not checked (not verified).
- **Motion recording.** On the Kaoss Pad, Pad Motion records about 6 s of finger movement on the XY pad, including lifts, and loops it. HOLD keeps the last position. FX Release adds a tail when you let go. https://www.korg.com/us/products/dj/kaoss_pad_kp3_plus/page_1.php . Elektron "parameter locks" store values per step: hold a step and turn a knob. In live record, knob moves are written to steps. Steps where the knob did not move are left unlocked, which forum users find annoying. https://www.elektronauts.com/t/m-c-automation-on-all-trigs/146511
- SPD-SX, MPC, Maschine and Strike were not researched (not verified).

### 2.5 Turntables and platters

- **Digital vinyl systems (DVS).** Serato control vinyl carries a 1 kHz tone, which gives speed and direction, plus a position "NoiseMap". The modes are *Absolute* (needle position equals track position, so needle drops work), *Relative* (motion only) and *Internal*. https://djtechreviews.com/guides/tutorials/guide-to-digital-vinyl-systems . Mixxx recommends latency of 10 ms or less for scratching. https://manual.mixxx.org/2.5/en/chapters/vinyl_control.html . Serato says that with motorised platters it matters to know *when* a movement happened, not only where. https://djtechtools.com/2011/07/20/behind-the-scenes-interview-with-serato-rd
- The Rane Performer has 7-inch motorised platters with braking tuned for backspins. https://www.rane.com/performer.html . DJ Hero was not researched (not verified).

### 2.6 Games

- **Guitar Hero.** Each note has a timing window, and the score multiplier rises every 10 notes in a row, up to 4×. Star Power works like this: hit every note in a starred phrase to charge 25% of the meter, then tilt the guitar up to double the multiplier. The meter must be at least half full to use it. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar_Hero . The width of the hammer-on/pull-off (HOPO) window changed how the games felt. In GH1 it was "basically the EXACT frame". GH3's window was so wide that the game became easier. https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Hammer_On_And_Pull_Off
- **Beat Saber.** It does *not* score timing. Each cut scores up to 70 points for a 100° swing before the block, 30 for a 60° follow-through and 15 for cutting near the centre. https://bsmg.wiki/ranking-guide.html , https://roadtovr.com/beat-saber-instructed-motion-until-you-fall-inside-xr-design/
- **Rhythm Heaven.** Cues are mainly audio, and you can play with your eyes closed. Some stages deliberately cover the screen to teach this. Its designers found that "a flick tied to a musical beat gives the player a reliable internal sense of timing". Failure is handled gently. Tsunku's complaint about other rhythm games: their inputs ignore the song's real accents. https://retrocean.com/nds/rhythm-heaven , https://note.com/mako8598/n/ncb97c09a1efe?hl=en
- **Thumper.** One button and one stick. The designers aimed for impact and speed over note count, and placed sound "inside the story space". https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/road-to-the-igf-drool-s-i-thumper-i- , https://www.gamedeveloper.com/audio/q-a-the-rhythm-violence-of-i-thumper-i-
- **Rez Infinite Synesthesia Suit.** It has 26 actuators whose vibration textures follow the music, with LEDs on the outside so onlookers can see what the wearer feels. They found that more actuators was not better, and that vibration near the heart felt unpleasant. https://www.rezinfinite.com/synesthesia-suit , https://hapticdesign.org/designer/file004_mizuguchi
- **Fuser (Harmonix).** Four stem decks. You score for dropping stems on the downbeat and for meeting crowd requests. The crowd meter *shrinks* if you swap records too quickly. "We're never gonna tell you your mix is bad." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuser_(video_game) , https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2020/06/harmonixs-fuser-wants-to-make-you-a-dj-but-only-up-to-a-point
- Synth Riders, Just Dance, Electronauts and Patchworld were not researched (not verified).

### 2.7 Visual music and AV performers

- **Thomas Wilfred, Clavilux (1919–).** The performer sat at a console of sliders with his back to the audience. Wilfred defined lumia as form, colour and motion, and ranked form and motion above colour. He wrote keyboard notations and played from them like scores. His home "Clavilux Junior" had a remote for tempo, brightness and colour intensity. https://www.getty.edu/publications/keepitmoving/case-studies/4-snow , https://hyperallergic.com/thomas-wilfred-lumia/ , https://econtact.ca/19_2/payling_lumia.html
- **Mary Hallock-Greenewalt, Sarabet (1919).** Her rheostat gave 267 gradations of dimming. She also used a foot pedal and mercury switches. She argued that "the chief leaning should be done on the darknesses and brightnesses", not on full-intensity colour. Her patent claims a separate fader for each colour *and* a master control over both at once. She also patented a light notation. https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2017/09/mary-hallock-greenewalt-feature , http://library.pyramidal-foundational-information.com/books/Nourathar.pdf
- **Oskar Fischinger, Lumigraph (late 1940s).** A thin sheet of coloured light ran just in front of a rubber screen. The performer pushed the screen into the light, so only what protruded glowed. Hitting the screen made it "shudder... like a big explosion of rippling light". A second person pulled ropes to change colour. Sometimes he played with no screen, wearing white gloves. http://www.centerforvisualmusic.org/Fischinger/Lumigraph.htm , https://www.centerforvisualmusic.org/WritingLight.htm
- **Scriabin, Prometheus, tastiera per luce.** The luce part has two voices. A fast voice follows the harmonic roots. A slow voice holds a colour for long sections; the F♯ light burns for 232 of 606 bars. https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.12.18.2/mto.12.18.2.gawboy_townsend.html , https://zigastanic.com/2015/11/27/scriabins-luminous-piano-and-the-relativity-of-synesthetic-experience/
- **Robert Henke, Lumière.** The same control signals drive the lasers and the sound. In one example, one LFO sets both a circle's diameter and a sine tone's loudness. He also fed laser control voltages back into the audio as glitch. Network latency and jitter between machines was a problem. His fix was a local copy of the laser generator used only for sound. He describes his role as a conductor: loop a section, add LFO modulation, then move on. https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/robert-henke-lumiere-lasers-interview , https://www.midifan.com/modulearticle-detailview-5854.htm . He calls frame-by-frame laser shows "cheesy". https://www.barbican.org.uk/read-watch-listen/from-the-archive-robert-henke-on-lumiere-i-and-iii
- **Henke & Christopher Bauder, ATOM.** 64 balloons on winches. Bauder ran height and Henke ran LEDs and music. They used three kinds of mapping. Strict one-to-one gave the "most breath taking" result. Synced-but-unrelated was "the least convincing". Henke used at most 4 MIDI clips, "the maximum that he can handle and really pay attention". https://roberthenke.com/interviews/atoms.html , https://rhizome.org/editorial/2009/jun/03/variable-frame-rate-multimedia-performance-at-mute . In DEEP WEB, 12 lasers track 175 spheres on winches. https://cdm.link/watch-breathtaking-fusion-laser-light-sound-deep-web
- **Amon Tobin, ISAM.** He performed hidden inside a projection-mapped cube. A Kinect let him "pluck invisible strings" to control the visuals. https://www.commarts.com/project/22824/amon-tobin-isam
- Ryoji Ikeda, Daito Manabe, Squarepusher and Tim Exile were not researched (not verified).

## 3. Twenty transferable mechanics

Each item gives the mechanic, its source, why it feels like an instrument, and a concrete laser idea.

1. **Dipping: pressure reveals a running pattern.** Source: Wessel & Wright. Why: timing stays perfect even if the controller is slow, and the hand shapes dynamics. Laser: each pad owns a beat-locked look that is always running but at 0 intensity. Pressure from 0 to max raises brightness and beam count. Releasing fades it out. The engine handles timing and the hand handles feeling.
2. **5D per pad.** Source: ROLI, LinnStrument, Push 3. Laser on ACCENT: Strike = flash peak. Press = sustained brightness. Glide (X) = sweep angle across the projectors. Slide (Y) = hue or fan spread. Lift speed = decay, where a fast lift snaps to black and a slow lift leaves a tail.
3. **In tune where you land, plus rounding.** Source: Push 3 "In Tune Location: Finger" and the Continuum's rounding. Why: no jump on contact, and the result is accurate but alive. Laser: touching a fader-strip never jumps the look; only movement after contact changes it. A "snap" knob pulls beam angles toward fixed positions (center, ±30°, ±60°) at a strength the player sets.
4. **Two-stage press with a felt threshold.** Source: Osmose. Why: two layers on one finger, with a clear boundary between them. Laser: the first 70% of pressure scales intensity. Crossing the threshold, with a UI "click" or haptic pulse, adds a strobe or ratchet layer at 1/16.
5. **Hit zone and hit density.** Source: Sensory Percussion. Laser: on a pad or drum, center hits fire the center projector and edge hits fire the outer projectors. The continuous center-to-edge position sets fan width. Hit density sets scan complexity, so rolls produce shimmering lissajous figures.
6. **Hand height inside a beam.** Source: Jarre's harp and Harpelaser. Laser: each projector is a "string". Break it (with a camera, sensor or on-screen strip) to fire that projector's cue. Hand height tilts the beam or sets brightness. Optionally the beam colour changes after the hit, as Harpelaser does at 400 ms.
7. **Cue advance with expressive timing.** Source: Jarre's harp may trigger the "next correct note" (not verified). Why: the player cannot pick the wrong cue but still owns the timing. Laser: a song mode where any pad fires the *next* authored hit in a pre-built list, at the moment and pressure the player chooses.
8. **Posture as mode, motion as value.** Source: Mi.Mu. Laser: making a fist freezes the current figure. An open hand releases it. Wrist roll rotates the figure, and arm pitch sets its height. Keep to 3–4 postures and use the fist for timing-critical actions.
9. **Gesture motion record.** Source: Kaoss Pad Pad Motion, Elektron live record. Laser: hold REC, then move the XY or fader for 1–2 bars. The movement loops, quantized to the bar, so the hands are free for pads. Touching the pad overrides the loop, and releasing returns to it.
10. **Layered light looping.** Source: RC-505, Rebillet. Laser: five loop lanes, each with record/overdub/play, stop, mute, undo and redo. "Start from nothing": build a set from recorded laser gestures, with each lane mapped to one projector or zone.
11. **Parameter locks per step.** Source: Elektron. Laser: on a 16-step strip, hold a step and turn the colour or angle control to lock it there. Only that step changes.
12. **Multi-playhead canon.** Source: Fugue Machine. Laser: author one beam path, then play it on 4 projectors with 4 playheads at ×1, ×½, reversed and mirrored. A single phrase becomes counterpoint across the room.
13. **Rings with different lengths.** Source: Patterning. Laser: each projector gets a circular step ring of a different length (16, 12, 7). The patterns drift apart and realign, so the show evolves without new input.
14. **Bounce and shape modes.** Source: Tenori-on. Laser: tap high in a projector's column to drop a "ball", which flashes when it hits the floor; higher means a slower repeat. Or tap 3–5 points to draw a polygon, and a dot travels it, firing a beam at each corner.
15. **Seed, decay, evolve when idle.** Source: Bloom. Laser: a tap places a beam that echoes on the beat, dimmer and wider each time. After N bars of no input, the system mutates the seeds slowly. This rewards restraint during breakdowns.
16. **Scrub the show like a record.** Source: DVS Absolute and Relative modes, motorised platters. Laser: a jog wheel in *Relative* mode nudges or scrubs the speed of the look's time. In *Absolute* mode, wheel angle equals position in a 1-bar figure. Touch freezes, a backspin rewinds the sweep, and letting go brakes back to tempo.
17. **Star power.** Source: Guitar Hero. Why: earned build-up, a dramatic release, and a cap on overuse. Laser: hitting the ACCENTs cleanly on the beat inside a marked phrase charges a meter by 25%. At 50% or more, a big gesture or pad unleashes a drop look that lasts in proportion to the charge.
18. **Swing arc sets size.** Source: Beat Saber. Why: it rewards full, committed motion rather than tiny taps. Laser: approach distance on a fader flick sets sweep amplitude, and follow-through sets tail length. A small flick gives a small sweep; a full arm gives a full-room sweep.
19. **Two voices of light.** Source: Scriabin's luce. Laser: a slow "home colour" voice on one fader, changed every few minutes, and a fast voice on the pads that changes hue per hit relative to home. This gives harmony to colour.
20. **Duo roles: form versus colour, or pressing into a plane.** Source: Lumigraph, ATOM. Laser: support two performers, one shaping form and motion and one changing palette and intensity. Solo variant: a pressure surface is the "light sheet". The deeper you press, the more of a figure emerges, and a slap makes everything ripple.

## 4. What makes something feel like an instrument

1. **Low entry fee, no ceiling on virtuosity.** https://cnmat.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/2002_problems-and-prospects-for-intimate-musical-control-of-computers.pdf ; Jordà defines "efficiency" as musical output complexity over control input complexity, multiplied by the player's freedom. https://mtg.upf.edu/files/publications/765598-NIME2004-Jorda.pdf
2. **Stable, low latency.** The target is 10 ms or less with jitter of 1 ms or less (Wessel & Wright). Jack et al. found that 20 ms, *or* 10 ms ±3 ms, lowered performers' ratings of an instrument, even when they could not perceive the delay. https://andrewmcpherson.org/instrumentslab-data/data/andrew/jack_am2016.pdf . Jitter matters as much as the average.
3. **Continuous gestures, not just events.** Treat gestures as continuous signals (Wessel & Wright). MPE exists for this reason.
4. **Instant music, subtlety later.** https://nime.org/proceedings/2001/nime2001_003.pdf
5. **Stable mappings.** "Programmability is a curse" and "smart instruments are often not smart" (Cook). A player can only build skill on something that does not change under them.
6. **Use existing expert technique.** Drummers, DJs and keyboard players already have the motor skills (Cook #3). LinnStrument builds on guitar fingerings, Sensory Percussion on real drumming, and DVS on turntablism.
7. **The software reflects the controller.** Sonami's test for when a controller becomes an instrument. Design the light engine around the controller's dimensions, not the other way round.
8. **Feedback at the hand.** LinnStrument lights its notes, Ocarina animates its holes, monome uses brightness tiers, and Osmose has a tactile threshold.
9. **Forgiveness.** Timing windows, rounding, beat-locked engines (dipping) and gentle failure (Rhythm Heaven, Fuser).
10. **Direct mappings over illustration.** One-to-one sound/light mappings land hardest. Things that are merely synced but unrelated feel weakest (ATOM).
11. **Constraint helps.** Henke kept to 4 clips. Thumper uses one button and one stick. Waisvisz complained that complexity got in the way.
12. **Music lives in time.** Waisvisz: the hand is "more precise with musical time". Rhythm Heaven: a beat-tied flick builds internal timing.

## 5. Anti-patterns and failures

- **Gorilla arm.** Holding the arm in mid-air tires the shoulder quickly. "Consumed Endurance" measures this and tracks perceived exertion well. Fixes include supported or elbow-anchored gestures and short raised periods. https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2556288.2557130 , https://hci.cs.umanitoba.ca/assets/publication_files/3411764.3445546.pdf
- **Latency stacking from cameras and wireless.** MediaPipe inference alone is 13–25 ms, and end-to-end reports are under 100 ms. Bluetooth MIDI adds 7–20 ms. The Seaboard measures 24 ms on USB and 30 ms on Bluetooth. Henke hit jitter on networked laser machines. A webcam is fine for slow continuous shaping but poor for hits.
- **Too many recognised gestures.** Mi.Mu warns that accuracy drops as postures increase. The Genki tap was "twitchy". The Osmose bends by accident unless stabilised.
- **No physical reference.** The theremin is famously hard. Flat touchscreens need visual compensation (Ocarina). Joué offers no visual feedback of control state (SOS).
- **Too much complexity at once.** Waisvisz's first Hands, Sonami's "bull riding", Fuser's obscure scoring, and Tenori-on's hard-to-control Bounce.
- **Too little agency.** Bloom felt "only barely interactive" to CDM. If the engine does everything, the player feels like an audience member.
- **Windows so wide that skill disappears.** The GH3 HOPO window made the game easier.
- **Spam.** Fuser's crowd meter punishes swapping too fast. We should do the same for button-mashing FLASH.
- **Audience distrust of automation.** People suspect the Jarre harp is fake. Hidden performers (Tobin) give up the visible-skill payoff that Heap wanted.
- **Wireless and batteries.** "Batteries, Die" and "wires are not that bad" (Cook).

## 6. Open questions

1. What latency and jitter budget applies to *visual* hits on lasers over IDN? Audio-derived numbers (10 ms, 1 ms) may be stricter than vision needs (not verified). We should measure this end to end.
2. Should the performer face the lasers, as Wilfred faced his screen, or the crowd? This decides whether feedback goes on the controller or on the projection.
3. Should pads be "dipping" (always running, revealed by pressure), triggered (one-shot), or switchable per pad?
4. Which hardware should we support first? A Push 3 or LinnStrument-class MPE grid, a drum pad with zones, a jog wheel, or a webcam? Each implies a different mapping model.
5. How much should be "cue advance" (mechanic 7) versus free play? Can the two coexist in one song mode?
6. Should we offer a scored practice mode (Guitar Hero or Beat Saber style) to make the instrument learnable? Would it teach the wrong habits for live shows?
7. Should two-performer play (form and colour roles) be a first-class mode?
8. How do we stop mapping drift ("programmability is a curse") while still letting users build their own instruments? Locked "instrument presets" versus an open patcher.
9. Can safety limits (zones, intensity caps, blackout) be designed to feel like part of the instrument's physics rather than an interruption?
